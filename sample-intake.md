## Executive summary

The request seeks to address party and matchmaking problems in *Frontier
Ops*, an online multiplayer game. The issue affects players on PC,
PlayStation, and Xbox. Players sometimes lose their party when they move
between game modes, wait too long in matchmaking, or get placed in a
match with players from a different region. The intended outcome is a
more consistent way for players to form parties and enter matches
together. Success is expected to be measured through fewer party
failures, lower queue abandonment, and fewer support tickets.

## Business context

- **Problem:** Players in Frontier Ops often lose their party when they
  change game modes or enter matchmaking. Some players wait in a queue
  and then leave because the match takes too long to start. Players in
  different regions can also be placed together, which creates poor
  connection quality. The current party and matchmaking services do not
  handle these situations consistently across PC, PlayStation, and Xbox.
- **Affected users:** The game has about 180,000 monthly active players
  across PC, PlayStation, and Xbox. Around 22,000 players use parties
  each day. In the last 60 days, Customer Support received 1,840 tickets
  related to party disconnects, failed invites, or matchmaking problems.
- **Desired outcome:** Players should be able to create a party, invite
  friends, change game modes, and enter a match together without losing
  their party. Matchmaking should use the selected region and keep queue
  times within the agreed service targets.
- **Success measure:** Reduce party-related support tickets from 1,840
  over 60 days to fewer than 900 over a similar 60-day period after
  launch. Reduce queue abandonment for waits longer than 3 minutes from
  18% to below 10%. Keep 95% of party members together when a match is
  found.
- **Strategic goal:** Improve the online experience and player retention
  for Frontier Ops. The work supports the Multiplayer team’s goal of
  reducing service issues and making group play more reliable.

## Scope and outcomes

### Scope

- **In scope:** Deliver a shared party service for PC, PlayStation, and
  Xbox. Support party creation, friend invitations, party membership
  across game modes, party leader changes, and party status. Update
  matchmaking to use party size, selected region, skill range, and
  available game modes. Add service monitoring for party creation,
  matchmaking queue time, match assignment, and failed requests.
- **Out of scope:** Rebuild the player account system, replace the
  game’s anti-cheat service, add new game modes, redesign the friends
  list, or change the game’s ranking and progression systems.
- **Definition of done:** Players on all three platforms can create a
  party, invite friends, change supported game modes, and enter a match
  together. Party membership is retained during the supported flows.
  Matchmaking follows the agreed region and skill rules. Service
  dashboards and alerts are available to the Online Services team.
  Support and operations have runbooks and know how to handle common
  failures.
- **First phase:** Start with party creation, invitations, party
  membership, and matchmaking entry for the current game modes. Add
  party leader changes and additional monitoring in the next release if
  the first phase meets its reliability targets.

## Technical and operational context

### Requirements

- **Functional requirements:** Players can create a party, invite
  friends, accept or decline invitations, leave a party, and see current
  party members. The party should remain together when players move
  between supported game modes. The party leader can select a game mode
  and start matchmaking. The service should handle a player
  disconnecting and rejoining where supported. Matchmaking should
  consider party size, region, skill range, and game mode. The system
  should return clear error messages when a party cannot enter a match.
- **Integrations and data:** The service will connect to the existing
  player account service, friends service, game session service,
  matchmaking service, platform identity services, and telemetry
  pipeline. It will use player account IDs, platform IDs, party
  membership, selected region, game mode, matchmaking status, and
  service event data.
- **Non-functional requirements:** Support a peak of 120,000 concurrent
  players across all regions. Party actions should complete within 500
  ms for 95% of requests, excluding platform network delays. The service
  target is 99.9% monthly availability. The service must support
  monitoring, alerting, audit logs for key party actions, and a
  documented recovery process.

### Technical context

- **Systems affected:** Player account service, friends service, game
  session service, matchmaking service, platform identity services for
  PC, PlayStation, and Xbox, and the telemetry pipeline.

## Risks and rollout

- **Known risks:** The three platform identity services may behave
  differently when a player changes platform or reconnects. A
  matchmaking change could increase queue times in regions with fewer
  players. The new service may receive more party requests than the
  current service during a major content release. The team should run
  cross-platform tests, load tests, and a staged rollout before enabling
  the service for all players.
- **Assumptions:** Existing player accounts and friend relationships can
  be used by the new service. Platform identity services will provide
  the required player identifiers. The current game session service can
  support the new party flow without a full replacement.
- **Constraints:** The feature must work with the current PC,
  PlayStation, and Xbox releases. The team must meet platform
  certification requirements and the planned content release date. The
  existing account and anti-cheat services are not being replaced.
- **Rollout and adoption:** Run an internal test first, followed by a
  limited rollout in one region. Monitor party failures, queue time,
  matchmaking errors, and support tickets. Increase rollout by region
  after the service meets the agreed targets. Prepare support guidance,
  operational runbooks, and an incident response contact list before the
  global release.
- **Priority context:** The work is tied to the 15 September 2027
  content release. If it misses that date, the current party and
  matchmaking problems will remain for the release period. The team
  should confirm the required capacity and technical dependencies before
  committing to the delivery date.
